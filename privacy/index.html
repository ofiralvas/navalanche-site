Navalanche

Privacy Policy

Effective 9 November 2026 · Applies to the Navalanche app for Android, iPhone and web

The short version
  • You don't give us your name, email or phone number. Your account is an anonymous ID.
  • We use your location to show you to your accepted friends on the mountain, including while the app is in the background. Turn on Ghost mode and your location is removed from our server.
  • We keep only your latest position on our server, not a history of where you've been.
  • We don't sell your data, we don't show ads, and we don't track you across other apps.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it from the app, or by emailing us.

Who we are

Navalanche is made by Ofir Alvas, an independent developer (the "data controller"). Questions, requests or complaints: ofir.alvas@gmail.com. We aim to reply within 7 days and to complete any request within 30 days.

What we collect and why

Location (precise, including in the background)

When you allow it, the app reads your phone's GPS: latitude, longitude, altitude, heading and speed. We send your latest position to our server so your accepted friends can see you on the map, find you, and get a "wave" when you're nearby. If you allow "Allow all the time" (Android) or "Always" (iPhone), the app keeps updating your position while it's in your pocket, and on Android you'll see a "Navalanche is live" notification while that happens.

  • Who sees it: only riders you have accepted as friends, and only while Ghost mode is off.
  • How long: each new position replaces the previous one; we don't store a trail. Your last position stays on the server until it's replaced, until you turn on Ghost mode (which deletes it), or until you delete your account.
  • Your daily ride stats (distance, runs, vertical, top speed) are calculated on your phone and are not uploaded.
  • When you ask for a route, the start and end points are sent to our server to calculate the route and are not saved.

Your profile

A username, optional bio, skill level, ski or snowboard, and an optional profile photo. This is so friends can recognise you. Any Navalanche user can see your profile (so they can find and add you). Your profile photo is stored at a public web address: anyone who has that link can open it.

Messages, voice messages and groups

Text messages, walkie-talkie voice messages, waves, the groups you create or join, and group meeting points. They're stored so the people in that chat or group can read and play them. The microphone is used only while you hold the record button. Messages are encrypted in transit but are not end-to-end encrypted; they're stored on our server.

Friends and blocks

Who you've invited, accepted or blocked. This decides who can see your location.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, we store a push token for your device. When someone messages you, the sender's name and a preview of the message (up to 140 characters, or "Voice message") is passed through Expo's push service and then Google or Apple to reach your phone.

Motion sensors

The app reads the accelerometer while you ride to estimate the snow surface for your day recap. This stays on your phone.

Crash reports and app usage

If the app crashes, a crash report (error details, phone model, operating system, app version, and your anonymous account ID) is sent to Sentry so we can fix it. We also record a few basic usage events, such as "app opened" or "ride started", with your anonymous account ID, using PostHog. Neither contains your location.

Reports

If you report another rider, we store your report (who, reason, your note) so we can review it.

What we don't do

  • No selling or renting of personal data.
  • No advertising, no advertising ID, no tracking across other companies' apps or websites.
  • No collection of your contacts, email, phone number or real name.

Where your data is stored

Our database and file storage (your profile, photo, location, messages and voice messages) are hosted by Supabase on servers in Sydney, Australia. Crash reports (Sentry) and usage events (PostHog) are processed in the European Union. Notification and map providers (Expo, Google, Apple, Mapbox) may process data in the United States and other countries. If you live outside Australia, this means your data is transferred abroad. Where required, these providers rely on standard contractual clauses or equivalent safeguards.

We don't keep backups of our database. When data is deleted, it is gone and can't be restored.

Legal basis (EU / UK users)

We process your data to provide the service you asked for (contract), with your consent for location, background location, microphone, photos and notifications (you can withdraw consent at any time in your phone's settings or with Ghost mode), and on our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and safe (crash reports, usage statistics, abuse reports).

How long we keep data

  • Location: latest position only, replaced on each update; deleted by Ghost mode or account deletion.
  • Profile, messages, voice messages, groups, friends: until you delete your account.
  • Crash reports and usage events: kept only as long as needed to fix problems and understand how the app is used. We'll add the exact periods here once confirmed.
  • Reports about other riders: deleted after 12 months.
  • Backups: none. Deleted data is not kept anywhere else.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Account → Delete my account. This permanently deletes your profile, photo, location, messages, voice messages, friends, groups you created, and push tokens. Full instructions, including how to ask by email if you no longer have the app: navalanche account deletion.

Uninstalling the app does not delete your account. Please delete it in the app first.

Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, to delete it, or to stop processing it, and (in the EU/UK) you can complain to your local data protection authority. Email ofir.alvas@gmail.com. Because accounts are anonymous, we'll ask for your username and the Account ID shown in Settings to make sure it's you.

Children

Navalanche is not intended for children under 13 (under 16 in the EU), and we don't knowingly collect their data. If you think a child has an account, email us and we'll delete it.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Server access is restricted by per-user rules so riders can read only what they're allowed to see. No system is perfectly secure; we'll tell affected users and authorities about a breach where the law requires it.

Changes

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top, and for significant changes we'll tell you in the app.
